Six projects, each one you can actually run

Every project below has a live demo that runs in this browser tab — real Python, real models, real data. Move a slider and the analysis recomputes in front of you.

Each demo boots a Python runtime in your browser via WebAssembly. The first load takes roughly 10–20 seconds; after that it is cached. Nothing is uploaded and no server is involved — the computation happens on your machine.

Projects

Race strategy · live metrics · telemetry

Race Control — a Grand Prix, replayed lap by lap

A pit-wall timing screen for the 2023 Spanish Grand Prix. Play the race and the order, gaps, tyre ages and lap times update the way they would on the wall; four models run alongside it — lap-time prediction from a partial lap, tyre degradation, an anomaly flag, and a pit-window estimate. Click any driver to follow them around the circuit.

66 laps · 20 drivers · 1,312 lap records · 4 models, each scored only on laps after the ones it learned from

A passenger assistant for an autonomous shuttle. Type a question and watch the classifier decide what you want — or refuse, if it isn't confident enough. The refusal threshold is a slider, so you can see what accuracy costs in coverage and the other way round.

A driver says the car feels wrong and telemetry is the only evidence. A diagnosis only exists relative to a reference, so this compares against a teammate and against the driver's own earlier laps — and shows where those two answers disagree.

Recommender systems

Who does the recommender fail?

MovieLens is the most-worked dataset on the internet, and RMSE is close to useless on it. Two models two percent apart on error turn out to be wildly different in what they actually recommend, and the average hides exactly which users get failed.

2% apart on RMSE, ~69× apart on precision@10
