01Telemetry
Replay · 4 models

Race Control

A race weekend produces more numbers than anyone can read while it is happening. So I rebuilt the 2023 Spanish Grand Prix as something that unfolds — press play and the order, gaps, tyre ages and lap times advance the way they would on the pit wall, with four models trying to say something useful before the lap is over.

What it does

Turns a finished session into 1,312 ordered lap states, then predicts lap times from a partial lap, fits tyre degradation, flags anomalous laps, and estimates the pit window. Click a driver to follow them around the circuit.

Why this race

Barcelona 2023 ran without a single safety car, which is exactly why the degradation fit is clean: nothing neutralised the field and confounded the pace.

The honest bit

The lap-time model only earns its keep once two sectors are already in. At the start of a lap it wins 44% of the time against the baseline — a coin flip. The card shows all three states rather than the flattering one.

02NLP
Intent · refusal

A Shuttle Assistant That Refuses

Anyone can build the half that routes a question to an answer. The half that decides this one is not mine is the half that determines whether the thing can go on a vehicle carrying people.

What it does

Type a question. A classifier — fitted in your browser when the page loads — routes it to a handler that answers from a real timetable, or refuses. A slider moves the confidence threshold so you can watch accuracy and coverage trade against each other.

The data

CLINC150, which exists precisely because it ships a deliberate out-of-scope set, plus the open GTFS feed for a free town shuttle in Avon, Colorado. Both public, both attributed.

The honest bit

Ask it "my chest hurts". It refuses — at 0.988 confidence, with the safety handler scoring 0.008. The keyword tripwire meant to catch that case misses it too. A model this confident about the wrong thing is the argument for the refusal layer.

03Fairness
Interpretability

Who Pays for the Mistakes

Every credit-default project on the internet ends at an AUC in the high 0.70s. But a probability is not a decision — somebody has to draw a line, and the moment that line exists it hands a bill to somebody.

What it does

Set how much more a missed default costs than a false alarm, and the threshold, the money and the burden across demographic groups all move together. LIME and SHAP explain individual calls; fairlearn audits the groups.

Where it came from

A rebuild on public UCI data of an analysis first done for a private recruiting case. Only the method carried over — no proprietary data, values or results.

The honest bit

I first read four real disparities. A permutation test — labels shuffled 5,000 times with the model and threshold held fixed — put the true-positive gap by sex at p = 0.29. Three of the four survived. That one was noise, and the test now ships with the repo.

04Quant
Stat arb

Dies Once You Pay For It

Two funds track roughly the same thing. Their prices wander apart. If the gap is stationary, it is a forecast. The textbook version of this works — and that is exactly what makes it worth publishing the part where it doesn't.

What it does

Picks pairs on a formation window that never touches the trading window, tests cointegration, trades the z-score, and lets you re-price the whole backtest at any cost assumption with a slider.

Why the separation matters

Choosing pairs on data you then trade is the most common way this strategy is accidentally made to look profitable. Fourteen deliberately injected faults all trip the test suite.

The honest bit

Gross Sharpe −0.3560, net −0.5188 after five basis points a side and fifty a year of borrow. The spread mean-reverts exactly as theory says. The book still loses money — and it was already losing before a single basis point was charged.

05Quant
Walk-forward

Only Works If Trading Is Free

I wanted to know whether a cross-sectional forecast of next-month industry returns survives being traded honestly — the model never seeing a day that had not yet happened when the portfolio was decided.

What it does

Refits on an expanding window every twelve months across 636 out-of-sample months, then lets you drag the transaction cost and watch a real edge become no edge.

How look-ahead was ruled out

Not by reading the code. Every observation after the cutoff was poisoned with garbage and the feature panel rebuilt — it came back bit-identical.

The honest bit

The answer was neither of the two I expected. The signal is real — permutation p = 0.001, rank IC 0.0578 at t = 5.82 — and the strategy still fails, because harvesting it turns over nearly six times the book's notional every year. Break-even lands at 35.8 bp.

06Diagnosis
Telemetry

Is It the Car or the Driver

A driver says the car feels wrong and cannot tell you what is wrong. You have twenty minutes before the next run and the only evidence is telemetry. Almost everything written with F1 telemetry is performance analysis; this is the question an engineer actually has to answer between runs.

What it does

Builds two reference frames — the teammate, which holds the machinery roughly constant, and the driver's own earlier laps, which hold style constant — then decomposes the lap corner by corner so the problem has a location.

The case

Verstappen's Singapore 2023 qualifying: Red Bull's one anomalous weekend of that season, and his worst qualifying of the year by a factor of 2.4.

The honest bit

The deficit is not spread around the lap. Turn 8 alone accounts for 49% of it. A loss concentrated in one corner invites a different explanation from one smeared evenly — but telemetry supports a hypothesis, not a proven mechanical cause, and the page says so.

07RecSys
Evaluation

Who Does the Recommender Fail

MovieLens is the most-worked dataset on the internet and thousands of notebooks end the same way: four RMSEs within a couple of hundredths, a winner picked on the third decimal. I came back to it with a different question — when this thing is wrong, is it wrong about everyone?

What it does

Compares four engines past RMSE: catalogue coverage, popularity bias, error by how active a user is, and cold start. Pick a model and a user segment and watch where the averages were hiding things.

What it ships

Only aggregate tables. GroupLens forbids redistributing the dataset, so no ratings and no user records leave the private repository.

The honest bit

Two models 2.1% apart on RMSE turn out to be 69× apart on precision@10. RMSE cannot see that, and it is the number everyone reports.

08Engineering
Pipeline

A Pipeline You Can Trust

Two satellite scenes, diced into super-pixels. Before fitting anything I wanted a floor — how much of the target does one threshold on one column already explain? All of it, as it turns out. The scenes were calibrated differently.

What it does

Pick a column, move the threshold, and watch a single stump separate the classes. The point is what surrounds that: YAML config, containers, artifacts that reproduce byte for byte, and tests that catch real regressions.

Why keep it

A trivial task is an honest place to show engineering. Pretending the modelling was hard would have been the dishonest option.

The honest bit

A row-boundary off-by-one meant the pipeline was reading 2,047 of 2,048 super-pixels, and the resulting 1024/1023 "class balance" had been written up as a property of the data. It was an artifact of the config. Mutation testing now pins the boundary in both directions.

How these run

Seven of the eight boot a Python interpreter inside your browser, so the models are genuinely fitting and scoring on your machine rather than replaying a recording. The first visit spends a few seconds fetching that interpreter. Race Control skips it and draws straight from an exported bundle.

Nothing is uploaded anywhere. There is no server behind any of this.

Colophon

The full analyses — code, tests and executed notebooks — live in private repositories. What is published here is derived: summary tables, scored predictions, return series. Where a licence forbids redistribution, the data stayed behind and the demo works from aggregates.
