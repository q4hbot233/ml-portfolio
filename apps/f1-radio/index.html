Team radio · 2024 season

Did the drivers have the race you did?

Readers rate every grand prix out of ten. Team radio is the only channel where you hear what it was like from inside the car. This scores every clip F1 broadcast in 2024 and puts the two side by side — after checking the score measures anything at all.

01

What you are actually listening to

a broadcast selection

Clips aired per grand prix

A grand prix produces continuous two-way traffic on twenty channels for ninety minutes. What reaches the public feed is the handful of clips F1 chose to broadcast. So this is not what drivers said — it is what was aired of what they said, selected in real time by people whose job is to make a race watchable. For this particular question that is less damaging than it looks: the readers doing the rating heard this selection and not the unaired remainder.

02

Does the score measure anything?

run before anything is correlated

A sentiment lexicon built for social media, run over speech-to-text of compressed race audio, has no right to be believed just because it returns a number. These are not the project's hypotheses — they are the conditions it has to pass to be allowed to test one. Each compares the score against something recorded independently of what anybody said: the lap chart. Intervals resample whole races.

05

Where the two disagree most

both sides as z-scores

A rating out of ten and a lexicon score between −1 and 1 cannot be subtracted as they stand, so both are standardised across the 24 races first. Positive means the fans enjoyed it more than the radio sounded.

06

Do teams sound different?

thousands of messages, not 24 races

Mean sentiment per team, raw and with the speaker's own afternoon taken out

This is the part of the project with real power behind it — a team's rows are hundreds of messages, not 24 races. It is also the part where the obvious alternative explanation is strongest: a team that had a good season sounds happier for reasons that have nothing to do with how it communicates. The adjusted column regresses each message on where that driver was running, whether they were up or down on their grid slot, whether their car was about to stop, and how far into the race it was — then takes what is left.

What this does not show

  • A clip is a channel, not a speaker. Most contain the race engineer as well as the driver. Diarising five seconds of clipped radio is not reliable and a wrong split would be worse than none, so the unit is an aired exchange on a driver's channel.
  • 24 races. A correlation from 24 points has an interval wide enough to hold most of the answers you might have guessed. Nothing in section 03 settles anything.
  • The transcripts contain real errors. This is in-helmet audio over engine noise through a compressed broadcast feed.
  • Ratings are RaceFans readers — an engaged, self-selected audience, not the viewing public.
