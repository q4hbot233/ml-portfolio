Team radio · 2024 season

Did the drivers have the race you did?

Readers rate every grand prix out of ten. Team radio is the only channel where you hear what it was like from inside the car. Every clip F1 broadcast in 2024 is transcribed and scored by a transformer, with a rule-based lexicon kept beside it as the audit trail. Before either score is correlated with anything, it has to show that it measures something at all.

00

Where every number here comes from

three sources, all public
Radio index
Formula 1's own live-timing host publishes a manifest per session listing every clip it broadcast. Fetched directly:
https://livetiming.formula1.com/static/2024/2024-05-26_Monaco_Grand_Prix/2024-05-26_Race/TeamRadio.json
The session path in the middle comes from FastF1, which also supplies the lap chart, grid slots, finishing positions and the race-control messages used to anchor each clip to a lap.
Audio
Each manifest entry names an mp3 under the same path:
…/2024-05-26_Race/TeamRadio/CHALEC01_16_20240526_150000.mp3
Downloaded into a git-ignored cache and transcribed locally with faster-whisper base.en. The audio is never redistributed and neither is the full transcript set; the 64 clips in section 02 are quoted, capped at 180 characters.
Ratings
Keith Collantine, "Ranked: All 30 Formula 1 races of last season rated by our readers", RaceFans, 2 January 2025. Transcribed by hand from the published article into one CSV with a provenance header; the site is not scraped. The six sprint races it also rates are dropped: a 100 km sprint is a different event.
Not used
No paid API, no scraping, nothing behind a login. Every figure on this page is recomputable from those three inputs and the code.
01

What you are actually listening to

a broadcast selection

Clips aired per grand prix

A grand prix produces continuous two-way traffic on twenty channels for ninety minutes. What reaches the public feed is the handful of clips F1 chose to broadcast. So this is what was aired of what drivers said, selected in real time by people whose job is to make a race watchable. For this particular question that is less damaging than it looks: the readers doing the rating heard this selection and not the unaired remainder.

02

What the scorer actually sees

One aired clip

Two scorers, on the same words. The transformer, a 125M-parameter RoBERTa fine-tuned on ~124M tweets and read as P(positive) − P(negative), carries every number on this page. The lexicon is VADER with 47 F1 terms added, 15 of them there to stop it reading box, push, hammer and attack as emotion when they are instructions. It is auditable to the word, which is why it is still here, and it is the weaker of the two.

Section 03 is where that comes from: the transformer passes the construct checks more strongly, and it does not read "yeah, tough luck" as cheerful. The two agree less than you might expect —

03

Does the score measure anything?

run before anything is correlated

The same three checks, on the other scorer

A score from a model that never saw a race, run over speech-to-text of compressed race audio, has no right to be believed just because it returns a number. These checks come before any hypothesis: a score that fails them is not allowed to test one. Each compares the score against something recorded independently of what anybody said: the lap chart. Intervals resample whole races.

07

Do teams sound different?

where the scorer choice starts to matter

Mean sentiment per team, raw and with the speaker's own afternoon taken out

The same ten teams, ranked by each scorer

This was going to be the part of the project with real power behind it: a team's row is hundreds of messages, not 24 races. Then the scorer was swapped and the ordering moved. Ferrari goes from eighth to first, RB from first to fourth, and the two rankings agree at Spearman . The correlations in section 04 do not move at all; this does. So the honest reading is that the per-team differences are real in size but not identified: they depend on which instrument you measure them with, and section 02 shows the lexicon losing on cases like "yeah, tough luck".

The obvious alternative explanation is also still live: a team that had a good season sounds happier for reasons that have nothing to do with how it communicates. The adjusted column regresses each message on where that driver was running, whether they were up or down on their grid slot, whether the car was about to stop, and how far into the race it was, then takes what is left.

Caveats

  • A clip is a channel, not a speaker. Most contain the race engineer as well as the driver. Diarising five seconds of clipped radio is not reliable and a wrong split would be worse than none, so the unit is an aired exchange on a driver's channel.
  • 24 races. A correlation from 24 points has an interval wide enough to hold most of the answers you might have guessed. Nothing in section 03 settles anything.
  • The transcripts contain real errors. This is in-helmet audio over engine noise through a compressed broadcast feed.
  • Ratings are RaceFans readers: an engaged, self-selected audience, not the viewing public.
