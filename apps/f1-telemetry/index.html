Can telemetry tell you who is driving?

Give a model where a car brakes, how it picks up the throttle and what gear it takes a corner in, and ask it for the driver's name. It will beat chance — and it will mostly be recognising the car. Everything here is built to take the car away.

The approach, averaged over every corner both of them ran

Paired by corner: teammates share a car but not a set of laps — one of them reaches Q3 more often — so an unpaired average would partly be comparing their session lists. These raw differences are descriptive. They are not what the classifier scores on, and a difference of a metre or two is well inside the spread of individual laps.

Are the style groups just the teams again?

k-means on driver profiles

Style map · two principal components

Every driver is the average of how they take a corner, standardised within each corner first. Thin lines join teammates.

If the clusters were a car signature wearing a style label, teammates would land together far more often than chance puts them there. They do not — which is the evidence that section 02 is not the car creeping back in through a feature that should have been excluded.

What this does not show

  • Separability is not skill. The pair that separates best is not the best pair, it is the pair whose two drivers do different things.
  • Qualifying only, one season, and one corner at a time — the hardest version of the question. Pooling a whole lap would score higher and answer an easier one.
  • Engine speed on entry leads for most pairs. Within a team the gear ratios are identical, so RPM at a point is a function of gear choice and shift timing — a real input — but it could also pick up an engine mode that differs between Q1 and Q3, and this data cannot separate those.
  • AlphaTauri ran four drivers in 2023 and is excluded: a mid-season change is not a controlled comparison, because the car was developed in between.
